# Term index

A reverse glossary of stapes: look up a word met in its documents or its code to
learn what it means here, where it is explained, and where it lives. A word with
several meanings lists each sense on its own line, and **bold** names in a
definition are entries of their own. Links point to the section that explains
the term; code locations name the package (`stapes`, `cmd/stapes`) or the
trainer's file (`tool/rip-train`) and the main identifier. The audio engine's own
terms, the sound, the voice, the sequencer, the rip and the rest, are in the
audio library's [term index](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md);
an entry here that names one links there.

## A

**accidental** (also: `acc`) — A pattern step's semitones off its scale degree, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#a)): `take` writes one for a key played outside it, and the grid marks it `#` or `b` after the degree. [Stapes §1](stapes.md#1-what-it-offers) · `stapes` `cmdTake`

**agree** (also: two witnesses) — The comparison of two **rip**s of one piece where no score is to hand, through the audio library's `MatchNotes` ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#a)): what both hear is likely; what one hears is to doubt. See also **truth**. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · `stapes` truth.go

**air** (also: `air`, a recording's noise floor) — White noise over 7.5 kHz, 45 to 65 dB under a loop's loudest half second, laid over a rendered loop of recorded notes alone: NSynth's notes, recorded at 16 kHz, hold nothing over 8 kHz, where 17 of the 315 bands' centres lie, and an empty top would tell such a loop from a recording. See also **loop level**. [Rip training §3](rip-training.md#a-loops-level) · `stapes` `air`, `sampledTop`

**arranger** (also: `gen`, arranged loop, `arrange.go`, `arranger`, part, `partKinds`, texture, `textures`, motif, double, left hand, strum, counter-line, break, swing, `corpusKeys`, `familyRanges`, `kind`, `part`, `instr`, `chords`) — What `corpus ... gen` arranges every loop with in place of the bank's patterns: a key and a chord progression over the 88 keys, a texture of a line alone to six parts (`drums`, `bass`, `left`, `chords`, `strum`, `pad`, `arpeggio`, `melody`, `double`, `counter`), each part an instrument drawn by family, its notes written in its register, entering and dropping out, swung, syncopated, resting and breaking, and played with accents, swells and fades, a feel off the grid and a drifting tempo; laid out as patterns the engine renders, a late onset in a step's `Late`. Its label says `kind` `arranged`, each event's `part` and `instr`, the bars' `chords`, and the beats where the tempos put them. Each rule has its test over a shared corpus of 2,000 loops' labels. See also **meter**, **overlap**. [Rip training §3](rip-training.md#arranged-loops) · `stapes` `arrange.go` `arranger`, `partKinds`, `textures`, `perform`

## B

**band frames** (also: `.bands`, `loop_NNN.bands`, `bandsLength`, layout, `LAYOUT`, `band_frames`, fine plane, sharp plane) — What the rip's models hear, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#b)): a frame's 315 bands three a semitone from G♯0 to 10.96 kHz in two planes, the fine one heard through windows that part each key from the next, up to 2.97 s, the sharp one through the analysis's 93 ms. A corpus writes each loop's as `loop_NNN.bands` and its manifest states their layout; a weight file and a checkpoint state it too, and one of another layout or of none is refused by name. [Rip training §2](rip-training.md#2-the-frames-loop_nnnbands), [§4](rip-training.md#4-the-input-four-planes) and [§9](rip-training.md#9-the-weight-file) · `stapes` `exportCorpus`, `bandsLength`, `writeRows`; `tool/rip-train` `ripmodels.py` `LAYOUT`, `band_frames`, `refused`, `train.py`'s `FrameFile`

**beat head** (also: downbeat head, `beat`, `downbeat`, bar line, `metered`, `beats` column, `readBeats`, `BEAT_BAR`, `--probe`) — The frame model's two heads, a value a frame each, that say whether a beat of the **meter** and a bar line fall within a frame, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#b)), which reads the tempo and the beats from them, and the meter and the bar lines from them beside the cues, where a weight file has both. The trainer reads every loop's from the `beats` and `downbeats` its label lists, a beat the meter's own note: a rendered loop's where its tempos put them (audio's `BeatStarts`), a recording's where a `corpus <dir> from` list's `beats` column names their file, a JAMS file's `beat_position` annotation or a MIDI file whose bars are the recording's; `metered` marks the frames asked, a rendered loop's all but its last two, a recording's from its first listed beat's mark to its last's, and none of an **overlap**, a **shuffled window** or one drums are laid **over**. The loss carries no positive weight, since audio reads each head's level as a posterior, the downbeat head's beside the cues; the held-out and validation lines give each head's F1 at its peaks over a half (`BEAT_BAR`), and `--init` grows the heads on a model from before them, which `--probe` then trains alone, every other weight held as it was. [Rip training §3, §5 and §8](rip-training.md#a-recordings-beats) · [rip-train README](../tool/rip-train/README.md#the-beat-and-the-bar-line) · `stapes` `readBeats`, `corpusLoop`; `tool/rip-train` `train.py`'s `Label`, `losses`, `evaluate`

**binaural bed** (also: bed, binaural beat, carrier, `binaural`) — The tone the audio library lays under the music ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#b)): a sine in each ear, the two a beat apart around a carrier, which headphones give as a binaural beat. The `binaural` verb lays one under what the transport plays, by its carrier and beat in Hz and its level of full scale, says what is laid, and takes it away; it sounds while the music does and is in no render, export or corpus. [Stapes §3](stapes.md#3-midi) · `stapes` `cmdBinaural`

**bright** — A **rip**'s brightness of a note, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#b)), which `notes` prints, counting the unmeasured. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · `stapes` `cmdNotes`

**builder** (also: `--builders`, `Batches`, `stream`) — A process forked from a training run that builds its batches beside the device, at most two ahead, handed over in index order; batch k draws from a stream of the run's seed, its **shard** and k alone, so it is the same whatever builds it and a resumed run draws what the unbroken run drew. [Rip training §8](rip-training.md#8-a-training-step) · `tool/rip-train` `train.Batches`, `train.stream`

**by-string file** (also: `strings` column, hexaphonic, `hearPickup`, `hearStrings`, `heardEnds`, `readChannels`, `stringsOf`, `stringEndBound`, `stringBleed`, `stringFloor`) — A recording's second take, a channel a string from a pickup under each with the other strings' bleed taken out, in the order its **pitch track** names the strings, as GuitarSet's debleeded hexaphonic recordings are; a `corpus <dir> from` list's `strings` column names it, an empty cell none, labelled as without the column, byte for byte. A note takes the string whose `note_midi` annotation holds it and ends where its string stops sounding it, its partials told apart no longer holding more than is left unfitted around them on that channel, by hops of a quarter window (`heardEnds`), or at its string's next note; under `measured` a note alone by those ends is measured on the microphone, the strings saying nothing rings into it, and one with others gets none, a string's level straying from the microphone's note to note past the bound. The ends' bounds by kind are fitted on six lines of NSynth's guitars that hold the set's bleed and floor (`stringBleed`, `stringFloor`); a file gone, unread, of fewer channels than strings or at another rate is counted by reason (`unmeasuredWhy`). [Rip training §3](rip-training.md#a-recordings-strings) · `stapes` `hearPickup`, `hearStrings`, `heardEnds`, `readChannels`, `stringsOf`, `corpusFrom`; `tool/rip-lab` `labels/guitar.py`

## C

**candidate** (also: install, the four, a trade) — A round's file as it is read for installing, by [the rule](rip-training.md#11-reading-a-candidate): notes, drums, bars and voices on recordings no round trained on, under settings fitted for it, each holding, gaining or losing against what is installed beyond chance; a part that gains is installed without the part that does not, a gain beside a loss is a trade for Lixen, and a file that gains nowhere starts the next round. [Rip training §11](rip-training.md#11-reading-a-candidate)

**channel** (also: plane, `--channel`, `CHANNEL`, noise bank, `noise.npz`, `make_noise.py`) — two senses.
- *Input*: one plane of 316 inputs a frame the rip's models read: the fine plane's **band frames** in dB over the plane's level and the level, then the sharp plane's, and where a file has four, each plane's **PCEN** and their mean. [Rip training §4](rip-training.md#4-the-input-four-planes) · `tool/rip-train` `ripmodels.features`
- *Phone*: what a small speaker, a room and a phone do to a training window, drawn per window for `CHANNEL`'s share of them (`train.py --channel <file.npz>:<share>`): the lows and highs cut, a ripple across the bands and a steady noise floor added in power, shaped from a noise bank, two minutes of white noise as band frames and a real room's band shape, which `make_noise.py <quiet.wav> <out.npz>` builds. [Rip training §6](rip-training.md#6-what-may-change-a-windows-frames) · [rip-train README](../tool/rip-train/README.md#turning-a-run-toward-your-music) · `tool/rip-train` `Channel`, make_noise.py

**condition** (also: truth list's third column) — A kind of recording in a **truth** list, such as a phone's takes or solo guitar: the list's lines that name one are counted as one, a line that names none is a condition of its own, and `tune ... truth` scores a set by the mean of the scores its conditions carry (the notes' F1, and where a truth gives them the drums' F1 and the bars; the drums' too where its truths know that none plays, **drumless**; and the **readings** `weigh` names where its labels give them) and says each before and after, with its readings and those its labels give none of. `rows` writes each recording's condition beside its rows, a corpus directory one condition under its folder's name, and a **judge**'s fit leaves conditions out by name. See also **guard**. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · `stapes` `readTruthLines`, `conditionCounts`, `scores`

**corpus line** (also: `corpus.slab`, `pipeline.sh line`, `SEED`, `STAPES`, the round's corpora, notes folder) — The `corpus` verb's line a corpus is rendered by: with the build that ran it, and under `sampled` the notes folder it names, it gives the corpus's bytes, whole or in parts, so a corpus no pass reads is deleted and rendered again from the three. `pipeline.sh` writes it from its knobs, a blank between words, keeps it as `$CORPUS/corpus.slab`, prints it under `line`, and runs it by the checkout's build or `STAPES`'s on an empty root and config folder; `nsynth_families.py` makes the notes folder. [rip-train README: The round's corpora](../tool/rip-train/README.md#the-rounds-corpora) · `tool/rip-train` `pipeline.sh` `corpus_slab` · `tool/rip-lab` `datasets/nsynth_families.py`

## D

**drumless** (also: `drumless`, knows the drums, hits a minute, `drumScore`, `hitRate`) — A recording no drum plays in, as its **truth** knows it: a corpus label that gives no hit, or a truth list's line with the word `drumless` (a `.mid` or a transcript without the word says nothing of drums, and the word beside a truth that gives hits is refused). `tune ... truth` counts every hit a rip hears there as heard and not given, so it takes from the drums' F1 of the **condition** the recording is counted in; a condition that gives no hit at all scores the F1 of a rip that heard those hits beside every hit as many minutes hold at the hits a minute the list's recordings with drums give, one with none heard and less by degrees, and is said with the hits a minute heard. A list that gives no hit anywhere scores no drums. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · `stapes` tune.go (`truthTake.drums`, `truthCount.drumScore`, `hitRate`)

## F

**family** (also: `families`, `voiceFamilies`, `familyReach`, `nsynthFamilies`, `programFamilies`, `--family-holdout`, family head) — What plays a note or a hit, one of the audio library's nine ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#f)): `corpus` labels each event's as an index into its manifest's `families`, an engine voice's by its name in `voiceFamilies` while its genes stay within `familyReach` (0.2 RMS) of the registered voice on its wave, else the nearest registered voice's within that reach, else unknown, as is a voice `aug` drives; NSynth's notes by their instrument's family (`nsynthFamilies`), every hit and strike the drums', and a `corpus <dir> from` recording's notes by its list's `family` column, or where the cell is empty or absent each by its MIDI program (`programFamilies`, a note of no program unlabelled). The frame model's family head learns the highest event sounding's and the notes model's the family of each cell's note (**instrument model**); `train.py --family-holdout <family>` tells no head one family's name, its notes trained on as sound, and the share their surest name reaches is the **measure**. See also **instrument model**. [Rip training §3, §5 and §8](rip-training.md#3-the-labels-loop_nnnjson-and-corpusjson) · `stapes` `voiceFamily`, `nsynthFamilies`, `programFamilies`; `tool/rip-train` `train.py`'s `FAMILY_HOLDOUT`

**fidelity** (also: `Fidelity`, `songJudge`) — How much a render sounds like the recording it stands for, the audio library's grade ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#f)): `refine` judges the voices and the steps by it, the rip-back reported beside it, and `song <name> score` prints both. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · [Rip design §2](rip-design.md#2-stages) · `stapes` `songJudge`

**fits** (also: `fits`, fits directory, `fits.json`, `fitRow`, `fitLine`, `lineOwner`, `labelVoice`, `fitCounts`, `given`, own voice, held out, stopped short, prefers) — The voice fit read against voices that are known: `fits <dir> from <corpus dir>` rips and fits each loop of a corpus that kept its audio as `rip` does, or with `given` fits each voice its label states on that voice's own notes, nothing ripped, and writes a row a line, whose line it is by the label (the **voice** that plays most of its notes that match a labelled note, and its share of them), the label's wave and the fitted one, and the fit's own distance on the notes it listened to and on those held out for the voice the front end heard, the best seed, the fit and the label's own voice rebuilt from its genes, with the genes of the voice heard, the best seed and the fit less the label's. The label's own voice nearer than the fit is a search that stopped short; the fit nearer than it is a measure that prefers another voice. Its last lines count both, the lines of one voice alone apart. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · `stapes` fit.go, `Session.fits`, `fitRows`

**frame model** (also: `ripframe.bin`, `ripmodel.bin`, `FrameModel`, front-end model) — The rip's learned front end, a transformer over **band frames** that gives each frame's onset, drums, pitches, voice, family, beat and bar line, the audio library's `ripmodels.FrameSpec` ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#r)), and after them whether something of no pitch is struck (**struck head**), which audio reads nothing of: `tool/rip-train` is its PyTorch twin (`ripmodels.py`'s `FrameModel`) and trainer, and the **root**'s `ripframe.bin` the weights stapes hears with, beside the notes model's (**PitchNet**). A root's `ripmodel.bin`, the file's old name, is named at the start to be renamed and never loaded. See also **ripmodels**, **rip**, **training round**, **transformer**. [Rip design §2](rip-design.md#2-stages) and [§3](rip-design.md#3-learning) · [Rip training](rip-training.md) · `lixenwraith/audio/ripmodels` `FrameSpec`; `tool/rip-train` `FrameModel`

## G

**guard** (also: `guard <x>`, refused set) — What a set `tune ... truth` tries may take from any score a **condition** carries, its notes' F1, its drums', its bars or a reading weighed, against that score under the starting thresholds, 0.01 when not given: a set that takes more is refused, scored under every set that passes, so the mean cannot be bought with one kind of recording or one capability. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · `stapes` `guarded`

## J

**judge** (also: `ripjudge.bin`, `rows`, rows directory, `rows.json`, `rowStrike`, `rightStrikes`, `judge.py`, `--leave-out`, stage) — What rules which strikes of the notes model are notes, in the place of the accounting's installed scores, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#j)): a network a stage, the notes the decoder reported and the strikes it dropped, over a strike's row. `rows <dir> from <list|corpus dir>` writes the rows of a **truth** list's recordings or of a corpus's loops that kept their audio, each strike with its stage, its label (a truth note answers one strike, the nearest, a reported note first) and the installed verdict, a note begun at a heard start among the reported, held, which the fit leaves out and keeps; `tool/rip-train/judge.py` fits a judge on them by one stated procedure, counts the **condition**s left out, and exports the file; the **root**'s `ripjudge.bin` then rules `rip`, `truth`, `tune ... truth` and `live`, refused by name beside notes weights it was not fitted on. No fit ships yet. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · [a judge](../tool/rip-train/README.md#a-judge) · [Rip design §2](rip-design.md#2-stages) and [§7](rip-design.md#7-plan) · `stapes` rows.go, `Session.judge`; `tool/rip-train` judge.py

## K

**kept frames** (also: `frames <dir>`) — What the rip's models heard of a recording, kept by `tune ... truth` between runs as a file named by the checksums of the recording and of the weight files, so a later run reads it back in place of hearing; a change to the models' code under the same weights is not in the name. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · `stapes` `heardOnce`, `Session.earSum`

**kit** (also: `kits`, piece, `piece`, `kit`, `struck_genes`, `kitPiece`, `loopKit`, `kitLevels`, a piece's band, `strikeBand`, `riseBand`, low pulse, backbeat, time, fill, accents, `cast`, pass, `kitPass`, `renderKit`) — What an arranged loop's drums play on under `corpus ... gen kits`: two to ten pieces, each a **struck sound** of the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#s)) drawn evenly over its thirteen genes, at a level within the bank drums'. A piece's band is where its hit rises most, and its place in the order of the kit's bands is its role: the lowest the low pulse on the kick's line, the highest the time on the hats', the one half the kit up the backbeat on the snare's, those under it the fill and those over it the accents; every piece plays. A kit of more than four renders in passes of four, summed under one room and mix. A strike's label names its `piece`, the loop's `kit` each piece's genes and band, and `corpus.json`'s `struck_genes` the genes. The trainer reads no piece: a kit's strike is struck as any (**struck head**), and a loop whose label holds a kit asks the drums head nothing. [Rip training §3](rip-training.md#a-loops-kit) · `stapes` kit.go `newKit`, `strikeBand`, `cast`, `strike`, `deal`, `renderKit`

## L

**lead rule** (also: `rip ... lead`) — Which of the melody register's two lines a **rip** calls the melody, the audio library's rule ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#l)), which `rip ... lead` names. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · [Rip design §8](rip-design.md#8-decisions) · `stapes` `cmdRip`

**lag** (also: `lag`, `skip`, `lagReach`, `lagRise`, `lagBound`, `lagClear`, `lagDrawn`, `lagWhy`) — How far a recording of hits alone sounds after its file's hits, read from its frames' rise within half the usual gap between strikes: `corpus <dir> from` moves its hits and beats by it and its label states it, or says why none could be told, which puts the loop in the **skip list**; a hit of a recording placed that draws no sound is left out (**silent hit**). The constants are fitted on the engine's kits, each recording at a level drawn as a loop's. [Rip training §3](rip-training.md#a-recordings-lag) · `stapes` `placeHits`, `lagOf`, `TestARecordedKitsHitsAreMovedOntoTheirSound`

**label's heads** (also: `heads`, `labelHeads`, `labelHeadRows`, `corpusLoop.planes`, `truthTake.read`, `ripped.by`) — A corpus loop's label read in place of the heads the decoders read, of both models: the frame model's onset, drums, voice, beat and downbeat, the notes model's pitch, strike, release, velocity, cents, onset (`start`) and what plays each pitch (`plays`), a row a head, each written as the trainer's target of it (`Label.window`), the middle where the trainer asks nothing; the rest are the front end's, the analysis's where no model is loaded, and `all` is every one, which no model hears. `truth <name> <label.json> heads <head,...>` rips the recording again as it was ripped with them and scores that rip, and `tune ... truth <list> heads <head,...>` rips each label's recording so, so a decoder is read apart from the models that feed it. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs), [Rip training §11](rip-training.md#the-decoder-alone) · `stapes` truth.go `cmdTruth`, heads.go `labelHeads`

**labels pass** (also: `corpus ... labels`, `labels`, `bandsLength`, `loopFrames`) — A corpus's line run with `labels` after it: every label of the line and `corpus.json` written over those on disk as the line's export writes them, byte for byte, and nothing rendered, so a field the labels gain reaches a corpus in seconds. A label takes nothing from the render, its length counted from the loop's samples (`loopFrames`), and a loop with no frames whole on disk (its `.bands` header against its size and 630 values a frame) is labelled all the same and counted, `corpus.json` then left unwritten; a `sampled` line's recordings are read for their level and length, and a `from` line's recordings are labelled again from the list, each known by its label's `source`, and opened only where its velocity word is `measured` or its strings cell names a **by-string file**, a recording gone from disk or whose sound does not read then keeping its label with every note at -1. **loop level** (also: `level`, `loopQuiet`, `loopLoud`, `loopPeak`, `loopWindow`, `loudest`) — The level every rendered loop is laid at after its room, mix and codec, from a stream of its own whatever it plays: its loudest half second's RMS drawn evenly between -32 and -14 dBFS, lowered where its peak would pass -1 dBFS, and a peak that would hold it under -32 bent down to that. ## M

**measure** (also: `sure`, `--sure`, `SURE`, reach, where a name stops being sure) — The share a family must pass over a note for the note to be named: the highest share any name takes over each note of a family no head was told (`--family-holdout`), at the `SURE` quantile (0.95) of those notes, as a notes model's held-out line prints it. The run writes it into its weight file as a frame head `sure` of no weights, `--sure` hands it to the run that ships with every name taught, and audio names a note's **family** only past it; a file with none names no note. [Rip training §8 and §9](rip-training.md#8-a-training-step) · `tool/rip-train` `train.py`'s `noted`, `ripmodels.py`'s `PitchNet.sure`

**measured velocity** (also: `measured`, `unit` column, unit, `hearRecording`, `hearLevels`, `heardLevel`, `heardRing`, `heldSpan`, `stiffnesses`, `measuredBound`, `measuredApart`, `heardUnit`, `heardVelocity`, `lineTop`, `room`, `unmeasured`, `unmeasuredWhy`) — A recorded note's velocity read from its recording under the `measured` **velocity word**: the velocity at which the engine plays a note as loud, by its straight law, so its level over its recording's unit, at most 1. The level is a sampled note's, the RMS of its loudest held window of 50 ms (`heldSpan`), read from its own partials along its **pitch track**, each window fitted by least squares with the partials of the notes still ringing near them, a note ringing past its label's end at the pitch it was let go at for as long as its partials are found there and, in doubt, while what they could hold could move the measured level past the bound (`heardRing`), a window where it could bounding the level from above only; a partial the partials fitted with it widen past `measuredApart` widens the level's bounds, as does what the window holds that no partial explains, and a note known only more widely than `measuredBound` (0.8 dB, fitted on NSynth's notes laid one after another, the note before released, held on past its label or cut, and in overlaps, with no room and through `room`, which carries a note alone through a mix's room) gets -1, as does one another note sounds with and one whose level, bound or unit is not finite; a recording the measure gives no levels (`unmeasured`) is named, and counted by reason. The unit is the ninetieth percentile of the known levels of one MIDI program over the recordings a `unit` cell names alike, or the recording alone, over `lineTop`, the velocity the arranger's own draw gives a line's loudest notes. **stapes** (also: `cmd/stapes`, `.slab`, undo, stapes mobile) — The authoring tool over the audio engine and the banks vif plays: a REPL, a TUI over any terminal and headless `.slab` scripts for validation, apply and revert, audition, sequencing, TOML save and WAV export; a document saves back to its file, an untitled one to the **search path**'s first folder, where vif reads it next run. `undo` steps back over its edits, a command that changed a document each (a TUI key's commands one), each recorded from a shadow of the document as the last edit left it, the registry and transport untouched. The session, documents, verbs and TUI are the package `stapes`, which a host with its own screen and speakers runs in-process, as stapes mobile, its Android app to come, is to ([todo](todo.md#build-stapes-mobile)); `cmd/stapes` is its terminal command. See also **root**. [Stapes §1](stapes.md#1-what-it-offers) · `stapes`, `history`, `cmdUndo` · `cmd/stapes`

**starts word** (also: `starts` column, `startsWords`, `placed`) — What a `corpus <dir> from` list's `starts` cell says of how a recording's note starts were found, a row of one table: empty, heard struck; `placed`, placed on the recording by an alignment, as a take's notes from its exercise sheet are, which can miss a strike by frames, so the label says `placed` and the trainer gives its notes no strike target but a rise within the corpus's **reach** of each start, the onset head's and at its pitch the strike head's, their frames and cents still taught; any other word is refused. **struck head** (also: `struck`, `STRUCK_POS`, `STRUCK_BAR`, struck's F1, struck's peaks a minute) — A head of the **frame model**'s and the notes model's (**PitchNet**), one value a frame: whether something of no pitch is struck within it, a drum line's hit, a strike of no line or a **kit**'s piece, the frame of the strike and one either side marked as a hit's are. It is asked at every frame of every window, a recording's too, and a strike laid by **over** or spliced into a **shuffled window** counts only where it stands out; it joins the loss, a strike weighing 8 as a hit does in the drums head's, but neither `DECODERS` nor `READ`, and a held-out or validation line reads its peaks at 8/9 or over against the strikes as an F1, or as peaks a minute where nothing is struck. Audio from v0.34.0 loads a file with it and reads nothing of it. ## T

**training round** (also: round, `round.sh`, merge, `average.py`, `--init`, `INIT`, `MERGED`, trimmed mean, `TRIM`) — two senses. See also **frame model**, **pass that carries on**.
- *On one machine*: one run of the frame model from its corpus to its bench, `round.sh`'s three stages logged to `$CORPUS/round.log`; run again after a stop, it goes on from the checkpoint, which carries the best held-out step, drawing from its step the batches an unbroken run would, so it exports what an unbroken run would. [rip-train README: A round on a development machine](../tool/rip-train/README.md#a-round-on-a-development-machine) · `tool/rip-train` `round.sh`
- *Across machines*: one step of training the frame model over several machines: every **shard** starts from one checkpoint's weights (`train.py --init`, no optimizer state, step 0), trains apart, and `average.py` averages the shards' checkpoints weight by weight, after dropping each weight's `--trim` share of highest and lowest values, into the next round's start and a weight file; runs from different random starts are never averaged, since their units sit in different orders. [rip-train README: Training in parallel](../tool/rip-train/README.md#training-in-parallel) · [Rip design §3](rip-design.md#3-learning) · `tool/rip-train` `average.py`, `pipeline.sh merge`

**transformer** (also: `lixenwraith/transformer`, weight file, ALiBi, `Conv`, `Tower`) — Inference over sequences of frames in pure Go, float32, no CGO: convolutions over frames and features when a file has them, each pooled over the features (`Conv`), a linear input, pre-norm attention blocks whose heads lean on nearer frames by a slope per head (ALiBi) within a window, exact-GELU feed-forwards, a final norm and named output heads, the blocks one stack or towers that each compute a run of the heads (`Tower`); `Load` reads the versioned weight file `tool/rip-train` writes, tensors by name with an FNV trailer, and `Forward` runs it. Standard library only, a module of its own; **ripmodels** is its one user. [Rip design §3](rip-design.md#3-learning) · [Weight file](https://github.com/lixenwraith/transformer/blob/main/doc/format.md) · `lixenwraith/transformer`

**truth** (also: `truth`, ground truth, `timed`, bar row, `.jams`, truth list; see **drumless**, **label's heads**) — The score of a **rip**'s transcript against a ground truth, notes given as text, a tapped rhythm, a **MIDI file** or a corpus loop's label, the form the audio library reads ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#g)): how many of the notes given the rip heard and how many it heard that were not given, per line, aligned as **agree** aligns two rips, and for a rhythm the given notes cut in two and the heard notes spanning two; notes with pitches are also counted by the level the recording plays them at, in 6 dB bands under the loudest tenth, and alone or under another; a MIDI file's drum channel scores the drums; a MIDI file's bars, placed as its notes matched (`MIDIScore.Beats`), or a JAMS file's `beat_position` beats, a truth of beats alone (`ReadJAMSBeats`), score the rip's bar lines in a `bars` row (`MatchBars`: its bar's length over the given's, the downbeats on a line within an eighth of a bar when the two are the same length, the phase, the beats on a beat); a label's beats score them too, and its **readings** follow. The ground truth the fidelity and rip-back numbers lack: they grade the mix, not the melody. Its first row counts every note **by pitch and onset**, and `timed` takes a truth whose seconds are the recording's with no map. A truth list, a line `wav<TAB>truth[<TAB>condition[<TAB>[timed] [drumless]]]` a recording, the truth a `.mid`, a transcript or a corpus loop's label, is what `tune ... truth` scores the loaded models' decoder thresholds by: the mean of the scores its **condition**s carry, each one's F1 by pitch and onset, its drums' F1 where a truth gives hits, a hit heard where one knows none plays against it, and its bars where a label gives them, the mean of the downbeats on a line and the beats on a beat, under a **guard**; it then searches `onset_min` and the bars' parameters too (`drumGenes`, `barGenes`), and writes a parameter no score saw move as it stood. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · `stapes` truth.go, tune.go (`tuneTruth`)

**tuning** (also: `tuning`, cents) — The cents a recording's pitches sit off A440, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#t)): `rip` names it when it is off, an augmented corpus loop is rendered off A440 by up to a quarter tone, and the `tuning` verb shows or moves the transport's. ## V

**velocity word** (also: `velocity` column, `velocityWords`, `none`, `measured`, `vel` -1) — What a `corpus <dir> from` list's `velocity` cell says of a recording's MIDI velocities, a row of one table: empty keeps each note's and hit's MIDI velocity, on a kit the strength the set states in the engine's kits' measure, `none` says they tell nothing, as one constant over a set does, and labels each at -1, which asks the velocity heads nothing, `measured` says they tell nothing and labels each note at its **measured velocity** instead, a hit at -1, and any other word is refused. **voice** (also: tonal voice, `fit`, `genes`, `fitWords`, `fitOptions`, `fitDistance`, `mixDistance`, `fitScene`, `presentVoices`) — A tonal instrument as data, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#v)), which `rip` and `fit` fit to a recording's notes, `fit` at the sizes its words name (`notes`, `pool`, `generations`, `seed`): each window around a note it listens to is heard as the mix of the line's notes there on the candidate, the other lines' on their voices and the drums' hits on the kit, each at a gain of its own; `genes` says one's wave and genes as a corpus label states them. See also **rip**, **fits**. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · `stapes` fit.go, `fitVoice`, `cmdGenes`
