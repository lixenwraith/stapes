# Term index

A reverse glossary of stapes: look up a word met in its documents or its code to
learn what it means here, where it is explained, and where it lives. A word with
several meanings lists each sense on its own line, and **bold** names in a
definition are entries of their own. Links point to the section that explains
the term; code locations name the package (`stapes`, `cmd/stapes`) or the
trainer's file (`tool/rip-train`) and the main identifier. The audio engine's own
terms, the sound, the voice, the sequencer, the rip and the rest, are in the
audio library's [term index](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md);
an entry here that names one links there.

## A

**accidental** (also: `acc`) — A pattern step's semitones off its scale degree, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#a)): `take` writes one for a key played outside it, and the grid marks it `#` or `b` after the degree. [Stapes §1](stapes.md#1-what-it-offers) · `stapes` `cmdTake`

**agree** (also: two witnesses) — The comparison of two **rip**s of one piece where no score is to hand, through the audio library's `MatchNotes` ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#a)): what both hear is likely; what one hears is to doubt. See also **truth**. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · `stapes` truth.go

## B

**beat head** (also: downbeat head, `beat`, `downbeat`, bar line, `metered`, `beats` column, `readBeats`, `BEAT_BAR`, `--probe`) — The frame model's two heads, a value a frame each, that say whether a beat of the **meter** and a bar line fall within a frame, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#b)), which reads the tempo, the beats, the meter and the bar lines from them where a weight file has both. The trainer derives a rendered loop's from its label's `bpm`, `bars` and `meter`, a beat the meter's own note, and reads a recording's from the `beats` and `downbeats` its label lists where a `corpus <dir> from` list's `beats` column names their file, a JAMS file's `beat_position` annotation or a MIDI file whose bars are the recording's; `metered` marks the frames asked, a rendered loop to a frame short of its end's line, a recording from its first listed beat's mark to its last's, and none of a **shuffled window** or one drums are laid **over**. The loss carries no positive weight, audio reading the heads by rank; the held-out and validation lines give each head's F1 at its peaks over a half (`BEAT_BAR`), and `--init` grows the heads on a model from before them, which `--probe` then trains alone, every other weight held as it was. [Rip training §3, §5 and §8](rip-training.md#a-recordings-beats) · [rip-train README](../tool/rip-train/README.md#the-beat-and-the-bar-line) · `stapes` `readBeats`, `corpusLoop`; `tool/rip-train` `train.py`'s `Label`, `losses`, `evaluate`

**bright** — A **rip**'s brightness of a note, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#b)), which `notes` prints, counting the unmeasured. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · `stapes` `cmdNotes`

## C

**channel** (also: plane, `--channel`, `CHANNEL`, noise bank, `noise.npz`, `make_noise.py`) — two senses.
- *Input*: one plane of 230 inputs a frame the rip's models read, the first the bands in dB over the frame's level and the level, a second, when the file has one, each band's **PCEN** and their mean. [Rip training §4](rip-training.md#4-the-input-two-planes) · `tool/rip-train` `ripmodels.features`
- *Phone*: what a small speaker, a room and a phone do to a training window, drawn per window for `CHANNEL`'s share of them (`train.py --channel <file.npz>:<share>`): the lows and highs cut, a ripple across the bands and a steady noise floor added in power, shaped from a noise bank, two minutes of white noise as mel frames and a real room's band shape, which `make_noise.py <quiet.wav> <out.npz>` builds. [Rip training §6](rip-training.md#6-what-may-change-a-windows-frames) · [rip-train README](../tool/rip-train/README.md#turning-a-run-toward-your-music) · `tool/rip-train` `Channel`, make_noise.py

**condition** (also: truth list's third column) — A kind of recording in a **truth** list, such as a phone's takes or solo guitar: the list's lines that name one are counted as one, a line that names none is a condition of its own, and `tune ... truth` scores a set by the mean of its conditions' F1 and says each before and after. `rows` writes each recording's condition beside its rows, a corpus directory one condition under its folder's name, and a **judge**'s fit leaves conditions out by name. See also **guard**. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · `stapes` `readTruthLines`, `conditionF1`

## F

**family** (also: `families`, `voiceFamilies`, `familyReach`, `nsynthFamilies`, `programFamilies`, `--family-holdout`, family head) — What plays a note or a hit, one of the audio library's nine ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#f)): `corpus` labels each event's as an index into its manifest's `families`, an engine voice's by its name in `voiceFamilies` while its genes stay within `familyReach` (0.2 RMS) of the registered voice on its wave, else the nearest registered voice's within that reach, else unknown, as is a voice `aug` drives; NSynth's notes by their instrument's family (`nsynthFamilies`), every hit and strike the drums', and a `corpus <dir> from` recording's notes by its list's `family` column, or where the cell is empty or absent each by its MIDI program (`programFamilies`, a note of no program unlabelled). The frame model's family head learns the highest event sounding's and the notes model's the family of each cell's note (**instrument model**); `train.py --family-holdout <family>` tells no head one family's name, its notes trained on as sound, and the share their surest name reaches is the **measure**. See also **instrument model**. [Rip training §3, §5 and §8](rip-training.md#3-the-labels-loop_nnnjson-and-corpusjson) · `stapes` `voiceFamily`, `nsynthFamilies`, `programFamilies`; `tool/rip-train` `train.py`'s `FAMILY_HOLDOUT`

**fidelity** (also: `Fidelity`, `songJudge`) — How much a render sounds like the recording it stands for, the audio library's grade ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#f)): `refine` judges the voices and the steps by it, the rip-back reported beside it, and `song <name> score` prints both. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · [Rip design §2](rip-design.md#2-stages) · `stapes` `songJudge`

**frame model** (also: `ripframe.bin`, `ripmodel.bin`, `FrameModel`, front-end model) — The rip's learned front end, a transformer over log-mel frames that gives each frame's onset, drums, pitches, voice, family, beat and bar line, the audio library's `ripmodels.FrameSpec` ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#r)): `tool/rip-train` is its PyTorch twin (`ripmodels.py`'s `FrameModel`) and trainer, and the **root**'s `ripframe.bin` the weights stapes hears with, beside the notes model's (**PitchNet**). A root's `ripmodel.bin`, the file's old name, is named at the start to be renamed and never loaded. See also **ripmodels**, **rip**, **training round**, **transformer**. [Rip design §3](rip-design.md#3-learning) · [Rip training](rip-training.md) · `lixenwraith/audio/ripmodels` `FrameSpec`; `tool/rip-train` `FrameModel`

## G

**guard** (also: `guard <x>`, refused set) — The F1 a set `tune ... truth` tries may take from any **condition**, against that condition under the starting thresholds, 0.01 when not given: a set that takes more is refused, scored under every set that passes, so the mean cannot be bought with one kind of recording. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · `stapes` `guarded`

## J

**judge** (also: `ripjudge.bin`, `rows`, rows directory, `rows.json`, `rowStrike`, `rightStrikes`, `judge.py`, `--leave-out`, stage) — What rules which strikes of the notes model are notes, in the place of the accounting's installed scores, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#j)): a network a stage, the notes the decoder reported and the strikes it dropped, over a strike's row. `rows <dir> from <list|corpus dir>` writes the rows of a **truth** list's recordings or of a corpus's loops that kept their audio, each strike with its stage, its label (a truth note answers one strike, the nearest, a reported note first) and the installed verdict; `tool/rip-train/judge.py` fits a judge on them by one stated procedure, counts the **condition**s left out, and exports the file; the **root**'s `ripjudge.bin` then rules `rip`, `truth`, `tune ... truth` and `live`, refused by name beside notes weights it was not fitted on. No fit ships yet. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · [a judge](../tool/rip-train/README.md#a-judge) · [Rip design §7](rip-design.md#7-plan) · `stapes` rows.go, `Session.judge`; `tool/rip-train` judge.py

## K

**kept frames** (also: `frames <dir>`) — What the rip's models heard of a recording, kept by `tune ... truth` between runs as a file named by the checksums of the recording and of the weight files, so a later run reads it back in place of hearing; a change to the models' code under the same weights is not in the name. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · `stapes` `heardOnce`, `Session.earSum`

## L

**lead rule** (also: `rip ... lead`) — Which of the melody register's two lines a **rip** calls the melody, the audio library's rule ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#l)), which `rip ... lead` names. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · [Rip design §9](rip-design.md#9-decisions) · `stapes` `cmdRip`

**labels pass** (also: `corpus ... labels`, `labels`, `melLength`) — A corpus's line run with `labels` after it: every label of the line and `corpus.json` written over those on disk as the line's export writes them, byte for byte, and nothing rendered, so a field the labels gain reaches a corpus in seconds. A label takes only its length from the render, which the pass reads from the loop's `.mel` header; a `sampled` line's recordings are read for their level and length, and a `from` line's recordings are labelled again from the list, each known by its label's `source`. [Rip training §3](rip-training.md#labels-without-a-render) · `stapes` `exportCorpus`, `corpusFrom`, `melLength`

## M

**measure** (also: `sure`, `--sure`, `SURE`, reach, where a name stops being sure) — The share a family must pass over a note for the note to be named: the highest share any name takes over each note of a family no head was told (`--family-holdout`), at the `SURE` quantile (0.95) of those notes, as a notes model's held-out line prints it. The run writes it into its weight file as a frame head `sure` of no weights, `--sure` hands it to the run that ships with every name taught, and audio names a note's **family** only past it; a file with none names no note. [Rip training §8 and §9](rip-training.md#8-a-training-step) · `tool/rip-train` `train.py`'s `noted`, `ripmodels.py`'s `PitchNet.sure`

**meter** (also: `meter`, time signature, 3/4, 6/8) — A bar's time signature, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#m)): a step stays a sixteenth, so 3/4 and 6/8 are twelve steps. The `meter` verb shows or sets the transport's, with the tempo in the beat it is felt in when that is not the quarter (`Meter.FeltTempo`, as `rip` and `song` say it), which a **rip**, a **song** played and a **MIDI file** loaded set too, and `render`, `where`, the **mixer** and the playhead count in; a pattern's own (`meter = "3/4"`) lays out its bars in the **pattern editor** and in `cp <pattern>.bar.<k>`. `rip ... meter <n/d>` pins a rip's, and `corpus ... meters` and `bench ... meters` render a third of their loops in 3/4 or 6/8, the bench grading each meter apart. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · `stapes` `cmdMeter`, `beatsByMeter`, `benchTally`

**MIDI file** (also: `.mid`, score) — Patterns, songs and a **rip**'s transcript as Standard MIDI Files, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#m)): `write <pattern|song> <file.mid>` and `song <name> <file.mid>` write, `load` and `merge <file.mid> [name]` read, `notes <name> <file.mid>` writes a rip's transcript, `truth <name> <file.mid>` scores a rip against one, and `rip <file.mid> <name>` reads one as a rip with no recording behind it. See also **truth**. [Stapes §3](stapes.md#3-midi) · `stapes` `cmdNotes`, `cmdTruth`, `readScore`, `loadMIDI`, `writeMIDI`

**mixer** — The pane that draws every slot on one timeline, a row a track, and mutes a slot or a track or solos a track through `mute slot`, `mute track` and `solo track`, a silent track dimmed; the audio engine's mixer goroutine is in the [audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#m). See also **solo**. [Stapes §1](stapes.md#1-what-it-offers) · `stapes` `mixerKey`, `drawMixRow`

## O

**over** (also: `--over`, `OVER`, real drums, heard hit, `stands_out`) — The trainer's overlay of a corpus of drums alone, such as E-GMD's, on a loop with no drums of its own, for `OVER`'s share of the windows (`train.py --over <dir>:<share>`), at -12 to +6 dB of the loop's power and added in power; a hit joins the onset and drum targets only where what it adds tops everything else sounding over its best ten bands (`stands_out`), judged after the **channel**. [Rip training §6](rip-training.md#6-what-may-change-a-windows-frames) · `tool/rip-train` `sample`, `stands_out`

## P

**pattern editor** (also: editor, keys, grid, piano roll, take, yank, `n`, `e`) — The editor of one pattern, new (`n`, empty and playing along in the first silent slot) or edited (`e`), which its frame and header say, on two tabs: keys, two octaves and a third of a tracker layout drawn as a piano, each note of the key `key` set dotted, whose take is recorded over the pattern's length and written into it as a track a voice through `take`, each note on the degree of the key that sounds it; and grid, the tracks on a step grid under a bar ruler and the playhead, or with `r` one tonal track as a piano roll; `y` yanks the cursor's track, `Y` adds it to the yank, and `p` pastes the yank into the pattern open (`mix <pattern>.track.<n>...`), the tracks meeting at a length each loops in, or in the browser makes a new pattern of it (`cp`), refusing a yanked track that has changed since; `J`/`K` move the cursor's track down or up (`mv <pattern>.track.<n> <j>`), the order a build-up reveals the tracks in (`armReveal`). Every edit runs a command, so a script can make it, and `u` undoes what the last key did; `^D` twice deletes the pattern. See also **stapes**. [Stapes §1](stapes.md#1-what-it-offers) · `stapes` tui_editor.go, tui_keys.go

**bar phase** — Where a rip's bar lines fall against the music's downbeats, which the note scorer's time map never sees; scored against GuitarSet's `beat_position` annotations and a score's bars, [Rip design §7](rip-design.md#7-plan).

**bend** (also: glide) — A step's `bend`, the semitones its pitch reaches by its end from `bend_at`, a share of its length, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#b)); `corpus ... glide` writes it on generated notes and a rip's decoder sets it where the **cents head** follows a sound across cells. [Rip design §7](rip-design.md#7-plan)

**cents head** (also: `cents`, `corpus ... glide`, `glide`, `--even-glides`, `noteCents`, `CELL_HEADS`, stated error, `spread`, `CENTS_KINDS`, gliding, steady, beside, unplaced, `GLIDE_SPAN`) — The notes model's per-pitch head of two values: how far a pitch's sound sits from its cell's centre, and the error it states for that. A corpus label gives a note that moves its `cents` at every frame, its step's **bend** and its voice's **vibrato** as played with the loop's **tuning** in them, and one that holds sits at the tuning; `corpus ... glide` makes a share of the generated notes bend into the next or scoop and of the voices sway; a recorded note lists them from its recording's **pitch track**. The trainer asks the head wherever a sound the labels place, an engine voice's or a note that lists its `cents`, lies within a semitone of a cell, fits the stated error to the offset's miss with the miss held fixed, and prints the median miss, the median stated error and the share of readings within the stated one, for cells a glide is in, cells a pitch holds in and the cells beside a sound, and the stated error alone for a sounding cell the labels do not place. An engine note glides at the frames its cents differ from a neighbour's, a recorded note at every frame when its track spans half a cell (`GLIDE_SPAN`, 50 cents). Audio's decoder joins two cells into one bent note where their readings agree within the stated errors. [Rip training §3, §5, §8 and §9](rip-training.md#3-the-labels-loop_nnnjson-and-corpusjson) · `stapes` `glide`, `noteCents`; `tool/rip-train` `ripmodels.py`'s `CELL_HEADS`, `train.py`'s `losses`, `evaluate`

**instrument model** (also: the voice of every note, `pitchfamily`, `pitchwave`, `pitchvoice`, `--probe`, probe, `CELL_HEADS`, `GENES`, family head, unknown) — What plays, heard per pitch on the notes model (**PitchNet**): three heads a cell, a **family** of the eight names, a wave and the 32 genes, each asked where one event alone sounds in the cell, an engine voice the corpus labelled unknown as synth and `unknown` itself never taught, [Rip training §5, §7 and §8](rip-training.md#5-the-targets). Audio reads them into each note's voice (its `RipNote.Voice`; `notes` lists the wave and the family), a name only past the **measure**, and a line's voice is its notes' mean; a probe (`--probe`) trains the three heads alone on a notes model held as it was, so its file rips as the start's, [audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#n). What remains is in the [todo](todo.md#the-instrument-model). The frame model's family head, a frame's highest note alone, is read by no decoder.
**by pitch and onset** (also: within lines, `notes` row) — How a rip's notes are counted against a **truth** where a number picks anything: a note right when its pitch and its onset are, whatever line holds it (the audio library's `Agreement.All`); within lines, as the matcher pairs them, a right note on another line is a miss and an extra. `truth`'s first row and `tune ... truth`'s score, [Rip design §7](rip-design.md#7-plan) · `stapes` `everyNote`.
**judge** (also: the accounting) — The decoder's stage that keeps or drops each strike the notes model hears: today the accounting's fitted evidence, to come a trained stage on the heads around the strike beside it, fitted on truth-backed collections and read with a collection left out, in place of a bar set by hand, [Rip design §7](rip-design.md#7-plan).
**section** (also: part of a song) — A stretch of a recording between two boundaries where its tempo, density, level or harmony change by more than they vary inside it; tempo, meter, key and the voices are decided within one and never carried across, [Rip design §7](rip-design.md#7-plan).
**live rip** (also: `live`, `RipStream`, `Bars`, `Close`, `Final`, frozen bar, behind the sound, `pace`, the ear's wait, `FrontEnd.Ahead`) — The library's rip that follows a recording as it plays, each bar frozen once two follow it from the sixth bar on, `Final` the whole rip afterwards, its ear waiting the frame model's reach (2.97 s) so that its frames are the offline rip's, [Rip design §7](rip-design.md#7-plan). `live` drives it over a file in blocks, says each bar as it freezes with how far behind the sound, from the bar's end to where the sound is then, and measures the stream against the offline rip, [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs); the host to come is vif's Android app. `stapes` cmd_doc.go

**validation corpus** (also: `--validate`, `~/ripcorpus-val`) — Recordings none of the tests hold (GuitarSet's player 04, 19 MusicNet pieces), cut into fixed windows and scored every `--every` steps; the exported step is the best by that loss, a notes model's by its strikes' F1 there (`struck`: the strike head's peaks over the decoder's bars against the labelled strikes within four frames), [Rip training §8](rip-training.md#8-a-training-step); [Rip design §3](rip-design.md#3-learning).

**further parts** (also: `RipNote.Further`, `MIDIScore.Further`, every-part row) — A MIDI file's parts past melody, under and bass: in the transcript and the labels since audio v0.13.0 and stapes v0.12.2, matched in the melody register or, under E3, the bass's; `truth` prints an every-part row.


**pitch track** (also: `pitch` column, `readPitch`, `pitchTrack`, `pitchTrackReach`, `centsList`, `null` cents, `pitch_contour`, F0 file) — A recording's pitch as its collection annotates it, named by a `corpus <dir> from` list's `pitch` column: a CSV of seconds and Hz for one voice (vocadito's F0 files) or a JAMS file's `pitch_contour` annotation for each string beside its `note_midi` annotation (GuitarSet's), never an estimate of the audio. A tracked note lists its `cents` at every frame as an engine's moving note does, read along the track's line, `null` where the track is unvoiced, a sample is missing or it lies a semitone or more from the note; a `null` frame asks the **cents head** nothing. [Rip training §3](rip-training.md#a-recordings-pitch-track) and §5 · `stapes` `readPitch`, `trackOf`, `pitchTrack`; `tool/rip-train` `train.py`'s `Label`

**PitchNet** (also: harmonic stack, `--pitchnet`, notes model, `ripnotes.bin`) — The rip's convolutions-only model, the same weights at every pitch, over both input planes stacked by harmonic, [Rip training §4 and §7](rip-training.md#7-the-models); `ripmodels.py`'s `PitchNet`. Its weight file, the **root**'s `ripnotes.bin`, gives the notes beside `ripframe.bin`, [Rip training §9](rip-training.md#9-the-weight-file); audio's `ripmodels.NotesSpec`.

**PCEN** (also: `--pcen`, `PCEN`, `WARM`) — The frame model's optional second input channel, each band's per-channel energy normalization and their mean, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#p)): `train.py --pcen`, `PCEN=pcen` to the pipeline, trains a model of two channels, reading 128 frames before each window (`WARM`) so the average has settled where the window starts. See also **frame model**, **channel**. [rip-train README](../tool/rip-train/README.md) · [Rip training §4](rip-training.md#4-the-input-two-planes) · [Rip design §8](rip-design.md#8-measured-on-the-sample-recording) · `tool/rip-train` `ripmodels.features`

## R

**rip** (also: `rip`) — The audio engine's estimate of a recording, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#r)), and stapes' verbs over it: `rip` adds its patterns to the document and sets the transport's tempo, **meter** and key; `tune` searches its parameters with `lixenwraith/genetic` over rendered loops, or the loaded models' decoder thresholds against a **truth** list (`tune ... truth`), a tuned set read from the **root**'s `rip.toml`; `notes` lists its transcript or writes it as a **MIDI file**; `rip` and `fit` fit a voice to it, and `compress` writes its bars as a dictionary and a **song**, whose render ripped back is the score of the compression; `refine` closes the loop judged by **fidelity**, the steps' pitches moved and the voices fitted until a round gains nothing, each voice held within a tenth of its tonal floor, the fit's distance from the recording's notes. `render` writes what the slots hold to a WAV, `corpus` the loops a model trains on, clean or augmented, or recordings with the MIDI files they were played from, and `bench` grades the analysis and a loaded model on fresh renders. See also **stapes**, **frame model**, **song**. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · [Rip design](rip-design.md) · `stapes` `cmdRip`, fit.go, dictionary.go, tune.go

**ripmodels** (also: `ripmodels.py`, `ripmodel`) — The name of what holds all the rip's models, not of one: the audio library's package (`lixenwraith/audio/ripmodels`: `FrameSpec`, `NotesSpec`, `Load`, `Hear`, a `FrontEnd`'s `Frame` and `Notes`) and the trainer's module (`tool/rip-train/ripmodels.py`: `FrameModel`, `PitchNet`, `export`). Each was `ripmodel` until audio v0.25.0 and stapes v0.23.0, and `FrameSpec` and `Frame` were `Spec` and `Model`. See **frame model**, **PitchNet**.

**root** (also: `-config-dir`, `stapes.toml`) — The folder that holds stapes' own files, flat: `-config-dir`, or else `os.UserConfigDir()` plus `stapes`. `stapes.toml` is its settings, today `[audio] buffer_ms`, the mixer period, a key it has no field for refused by name; `rip.toml` the parameters `rip` listens with and `tune` writes; `ripframe.bin` the frame model's weights `rip` hears with, `ripnotes.bin` beside it the notes model's, and `ripjudge.bin` a **judge** fitted on those weights. The documents are not the root's but the **search path**'s. [Stapes §2](stapes.md#2-its-files) · `stapes` `ResolveFiles`, `Files`

## S

**search path** (also: `-config-music`, `-config-sounds`) — The ordered folders `music.toml` and `sounds.toml` are found in, today vif's audio folder alone (`os.UserConfigDir()` plus `vif/audio`), so stapes opens the overrides vif reads from its user root; a document saves back to its file, and an untitled one lands in the first folder, where vif reads it next run. `-config-music` and `-config-sounds` name a document's file outright. See also **root**. [Stapes §2](stapes.md#2-its-files) · [todo](todo.md#search-more-than-one-folder) · `stapes` `ResolveFiles`, `findDocument`

**shard** (also: `SHARD`, `--shard-seed`) — One worker's run in a **training round**, from the round's start on a stream of windows its `SHARD` picks (`train.py --shard-seed`), unique to the worker and the round. [rip-train README: Training in parallel](../tool/rip-train/README.md#training-in-parallel) · `tool/rip-train` `pipeline.sh shard`

**shift** (also: `shift`, `cmdShift`) — The move of every note of the tracks named, by octaves, by degrees of the transport's key carried into the octave, by semitones spelt again on the key (`PitchStep`), or by steps rotated around the pattern's length, a drum track only by step; a note past `ValidatePattern`'s step bounds refuses it whole. For a line an octave or a step off. See also **split**, **accidental**. [Stapes §1](stapes.md#1-what-it-offers) · `stapes` `cmdShift`, `shifts`

**shuffled window** (also: `--shuffle`) — A training window spliced from chunks of a second, each two clips summed, [Rip training §6](rip-training.md#6-what-may-change-a-windows-frames); `train.py`'s `shuffled`.

**skip list** (also: `skip.txt`) — A corpus's list of loop names the trainer leaves out, one a line, such as recordings whose MIDI sits off their audio further than a steady lag explains. [Rip training §3](rip-training.md#3-the-labels-loop_nnnjson-and-corpusjson) · `tool/rip-train` `load_corpus`

**solo** (also: `solo track`) — A soloed track of the pattern a slot plays sounds with the slot's other soloed tracks alone, a muted one never; set by `solo track` or the mixer's `s`, and dropped when the slot changes pattern. See also **mixer**. [Stapes §1](stapes.md#1-what-it-offers) · `stapes` `trackMutes`

**song** (also: `compress`, `song`, dictionary, matched kit) — A timeline as data, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#s)): `load <file.mid>` reads one from a **MIDI file** with its patterns, and `compress` writes one from a **rip** with its dictionary and the drums matched to the recording's, which its `kit` names, and `song` shows, its bars numbered as the paths `add`, `dup`, `mv` and `del` edit them by (`<song>.bars.<i>`), each with the meter or tempo word it names at its head (`"2/8"`, `"72bpm"`, a played take's rubato or a bar a score cuts short), renders each bar in its own meter and tempo (`RenderBars`; one line alone when instruments are named after the file) or conducts it through the live sequencer in its **meter**, each bar's patterns, meter and tempo scheduled to land on its bar line. vif's conductor places patterns by tier, not by bar; a song is stapes' form until a level wants a fixed piece. See also **rip**, **stapes**. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · [Rip design §2](rip-design.md#2-stages) · `stapes` dictionary.go

**split** (also: `split`, `cmdSplit`) — The parting of a tonal track at a MIDI pitch under the transport's key: the notes from it up stay, the rest go to a new track of the same voice after it, for two lines a rip heard as one; a drum track or an empty side is refused. See also **shift**. [Stapes §1](stapes.md#1-what-it-offers) · `stapes` `cmdSplit`

**stapes** (also: `cmd/stapes`, `.slab`, undo) — The authoring tool over the audio engine and the banks vif plays: a REPL, a TUI over any terminal and headless `.slab` scripts for validation, apply and revert, audition, sequencing, TOML save and WAV export; a document saves back to its file, an untitled one to the **search path**'s first folder, where vif reads it next run. `undo` steps back over its edits, a command that changed a document each (a TUI key's commands one), each recorded from a shadow of the document as the last edit left it, the registry and transport untouched. The session, documents, verbs and TUI are the package `stapes`, which a host with its own screen and speakers runs in-process; `cmd/stapes` is its terminal command. See also **root**. [Stapes §1](stapes.md#1-what-it-offers) · `stapes`, `history`, `cmdUndo` · `cmd/stapes`

## T

**training round** (also: round, `round.sh`, merge, `average.py`, `--init`, `INIT`, `MERGED`, trimmed mean, `TRIM`) — two senses. See also **frame model**.
- *On one machine*: one run of the frame model from its corpus to its bench, `round.sh`'s three stages logged to `$CORPUS/round.log`; run again after a stop, it goes on from the checkpoint, which carries the best held-out step, so it exports what an unbroken run would. [rip-train README: A round on a development machine](../tool/rip-train/README.md#a-round-on-a-development-machine) · `tool/rip-train` `round.sh`
- *Across machines*: one step of training the frame model over several machines: every **shard** starts from one checkpoint's weights (`train.py --init`, no optimizer state, step 0), trains apart, and `average.py` averages the shards' checkpoints weight by weight, after dropping each weight's `--trim` share of highest and lowest values, into the next round's start and a weight file; runs from different random starts are never averaged, since their units sit in different orders. [rip-train README: Training in parallel](../tool/rip-train/README.md#training-in-parallel) · [Rip design §3](rip-design.md#3-learning) · `tool/rip-train` `average.py`, `pipeline.sh merge`

**transformer** (also: `lixenwraith/transformer`, weight file, ALiBi, `Conv`, `Tower`) — Inference over sequences of frames in pure Go, float32, no CGO: convolutions over frames and features when a file has them, each pooled over the features (`Conv`), a linear input, pre-norm attention blocks whose heads lean on nearer frames by a slope per head (ALiBi) within a window, exact-GELU feed-forwards, a final norm and named output heads, the blocks one stack or towers that each compute a run of the heads (`Tower`); `Load` reads the versioned weight file `tool/rip-train` writes, tensors by name with an FNV trailer, and `Forward` runs it. Standard library only, a module of its own; **ripmodels** is its one user. [Rip design §3](rip-design.md#3-learning) · [Weight file](https://github.com/lixenwraith/transformer/blob/main/doc/format.md) · `lixenwraith/transformer`

**truth** (also: `truth`, ground truth, `timed`, bar row, `.jams`, truth list) — The score of a **rip**'s transcript against a ground truth, notes given as text, a tapped rhythm or a **MIDI file**, the form the audio library reads ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#g)): how many of the notes given the rip heard and how many it heard that were not given, per line, aligned as **agree** aligns two rips, and for a rhythm the given notes cut in two and the heard notes spanning two; notes with pitches are also counted by the level the recording plays them at, in 6 dB bands under the loudest tenth, and alone or under another; a MIDI file's drum channel scores the drums; a MIDI file's bars, placed as its notes matched (`MIDIScore.Beats`), or a JAMS file's `beat_position` beats, a truth of beats alone (`ReadJAMSBeats`), score the rip's bar lines in a `bars` row (`MatchBars`: its bar's length over the given's, the downbeats on a line within an eighth of a bar when the two are the same length, the phase, the beats on a beat). The ground truth the fidelity and rip-back numbers lack: they grade the mix, not the melody. Its first row counts every note **by pitch and onset**, and `timed` takes a truth whose seconds are the recording's with no map. A truth list, a line `wav<TAB>truth[<TAB>condition[<TAB>timed]]` a recording, is what `tune ... truth` scores the loaded models' decoder thresholds by, the mean over its **condition**s of each one's F1 by pitch and onset, under a **guard**. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · `stapes` truth.go, tune.go (`tuneTruth`)

**tuning** (also: `tuning`, cents) — The cents a recording's pitches sit off A440, the audio library's ([audio terms](https://github.com/lixenwraith/audio/blob/main/doc/term-index.md#t)): `rip` names it when it is off, an augmented corpus loop is rendered off A440 by up to a quarter tone, and the `tuning` verb shows or moves the transport's. [Stapes §4](stapes.md#4-the-rips-verbs) · `stapes`
